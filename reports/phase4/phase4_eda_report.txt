Q2. LENT EFFECT ON TUNA DEMAND
==========================================================================================
February–April weeks only (raw means):
           UNITS_PER_STORE  PROMO_SHARE
LENT_WEEK                              
False              973.892        0.182
True              1947.692        0.306
-> The chain promotes tuna much more often during Lent. Lent and promotion are entangled.

Weekly regressions, month FE + year FE + holiday dummies, Newey-West SE (4 lags).
Identification comes from Easter moving between late March and late April across years.

(a) Total tuna units per store
    without promotion control : +63.6%  (95% CI +18.5% to +125.7%; t = 2.99; n = 340 weeks; R2 = 0.46)
    with promotion share      : +50.7%  (95% CI +9.0% to +108.4%; t = 2.48; n = 340 weeks; R2 = 0.50)

(b) Products sold at their REGULAR price only (log units vs. own product-store average)
    with promotion share      : -5.1%  (95% CI -11.1% to +1.3%; t = -1.56; n = 336 weeks; R2 = 0.71)

Reading:
 - Total tuna demand is clearly higher in weeks overlapping Lent, even after holding the
   share of promoted products fixed (the interval is wide: only ~8 Lent seasons in the data).
 - Products sold at their REGULAR price do NOT rise in Lent (if anything they fall): the extra
   Lent demand flows to promoted items, which the chain concentrates in Lent.
 - Consequences: (1) the baseline/counterfactual model must include Lent features;
   (2) Lent is a CONFOUNDER for promotion effects - comparing promoted vs. non-promoted
   weeks without adjusting for Lent would overstate promotion lift.
